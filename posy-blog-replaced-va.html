i replaced $4,200/mo in help with AI employees that cost me $50

by brooke | the posy academy

i'm going to tell you something that made my VA nervous.

last year, i was paying over $4,000 a month in help. a virtual assistant. a bookkeeper. someone managing my emails. someone handling my social media scheduling. and honestly? i still felt behind.

not because they weren't good — they were great. but the coordination alone was a part-time job. slack messages, loom videos explaining what i needed, follow-up threads when things fell through the cracks. i was managing people instead of running my business.

then i started experimenting with AI.

not the kind where you paste a prompt into ChatGPT and hope for the best. i'm talking about permanent AI assistants — ones that already know my niche, my voice, my products, my stats. i set them up once and they just... work.

here's what they handle now

molly runs my morning briefing. she pulls together what happened overnight — sales, messages, anything i need to know — and gives me a 2-minute summary before my kids wake up.

frame reviews my VA's video edits. she flags issues so i don't have to watch every single clip.

nina schedules my pinterest pins. she rotates hooks, manages boards, and keeps content going out even when i'm at target with the twins.

and there are more. eight total, running 24/7, for about $50/mo in API credits.

the math that changed everything

here's what i was paying before:

  • virtual assistant: $800-1,500/mo
  • bookkeeper: $300-500/mo
  • email manager: $500-1,000/mo
  • social media manager: $1,000-2,000/mo

total: $2,600-5,000/mo

here's what i pay now for AI employees that handle the same tasks: ~$50/mo.

i still have human help — i didn't fire everyone. but my AI employees handle the repetitive, time-consuming stuff that was eating my budget and my sanity. the humans on my team do the creative work that actually needs a human.

but i'm not technical

neither am i. i'm a retired art teacher with four kids. i figured this out from my phone, mostly during naptime.

the tools have gotten so easy that "i'm not tech-savvy" isn't really a barrier anymore. if you can set up a TikTok shop account, you can set up an AI employee. it's honestly easier than filing your taxes.

what i'd automate first

if i was starting over tomorrow, i'd set up three things:

  1. a script assistant — a chat that already knows my niche and voice, so i go from blank page to ready-to-film in 4 minutes
  2. an email assistant — handles cold pitches, follow-ups, counter offers. builds templates WITH me using my own stats
  3. a live prep assistant — pulls product reviews, gives me objection handling, talking points, and saves for dead moments

these three alone would've saved me 15-20 hours a week when i was doing everything manually.

the real point

this isn't about replacing humans. it's about getting the admin off your plate so you can show up for the parts that actually matter — going live, filming, connecting with your audience, being present with your family.

i didn't build AI employees because i love technology. i built them because i love tuesday afternoons with my kids more than i love formatting spreadsheets.

work less. earn more. live more. that's the whole thing.
