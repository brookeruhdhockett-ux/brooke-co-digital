is AI actually sustainable? what i learned running 8 AI employees

by brooke | the posy academy

i run eight AI employees. they work 24/7. they never sleep.

at some point i started wondering: what does that actually cost the planet?

i'm not an environmentalist with a capital E. but i'm a mom with four kids, and i'd like them to have a planet that works. so i looked into it.

here's what i found — and why i think creators using AI responsibly is actually a good thing.

the honest numbers

let's start with what we know.

a single AI query — like asking your assistant to write a script — uses roughly 10x the energy of a google search. that sounds bad until you look at the actual numbers: we're talking about a few watt-hours. roughly the energy cost of keeping a lightbulb on for a few minutes.

running my 8 AI employees for a month uses about as much energy as leaving a laptop on 24/7. it's not zero, but it's not what most people imagine when they hear "AI is destroying the planet."

for context, streaming an hour of netflix uses more energy than a full day of my AI employees working.

what the headlines get wrong

most of the scary headlines about AI and energy are about TRAINING models — the massive upfront cost of building something like ChatGPT or Claude from scratch. that takes enormous computing power.

but using a trained model? that's a fraction of the cost. and as a creator running AI assistants, you're using — not training. the heavy lifting was already done.

it's like the difference between building a car factory and driving a car. yes, the factory took incredible resources. but your daily commute isn't the problem.

what the headlines get right

that said — it's not nothing.

data centers use water for cooling. they use electricity, and not all of it is renewable yet. as more people use AI, that demand grows. and the big tech companies building these models need to be accountable for their energy sourcing.

i'm not here to pretend there's zero impact. there is. the question is whether it's proportional to the value — and whether it's better or worse than the alternative.

the comparison most people don't make

here's what my business looked like BEFORE AI employees:

  • driving to meetings with my VA (gas, time)
  • printing contracts and paperwork (paper, ink, shipping)
  • running a home office with two monitors, a printer, and a desktop running 12 hours a day
  • my VA driving to her office, running her own setup
  • hiring a bookkeeper, email manager, social media manager — each with their own office, equipment, commute

the carbon footprint of a human team is real. commutes. office space. hardware. shipping. all of it.

my AI employees run on a server somewhere. no commute. no office. no printer. no separate laptop for each person.

i'm not saying AI is "green." i'm saying the comparison isn't AI vs. nothing — it's AI vs. the alternative way of getting the same work done. and when you look at it that way, the math is different than the headlines suggest.

what i actually do about it

i'm not going to pretend i offset my AI usage with carbon credits or anything. but here's what feels right to me:

  1. i don't run AI for the sake of running AI. every assistant has a job. if it's not saving me time or money, i shut it down. no waste.
  2. i chose providers that care. anthropic (the company behind claude, which powers most of my employees) has published commitments on responsible scaling. that matters to me.
  3. i talk about it honestly. i'm not going to pretend AI is perfect, and i'm not going to pretend it's the apocalypse. the truth is in the middle, and creators deserve to hear it.

the bigger picture

the way i see it: AI tools that help individual creators work less and earn more are a net positive. they reduce the need for large teams, cut down on physical resources, and give people time back with their families.

could the tech industry do better on energy? absolutely. should we pay attention to how data centers are powered? yes.

but should you feel guilty about using an AI assistant to write your scripts and manage your emails? no. that's like feeling guilty about using a dishwasher. it uses energy, but it also gives you back an hour of your evening.

the goal isn't perfection. the goal is being thoughtful about the tools we use and why.

the posy approach

a posy is small and intentional. each flower chosen for a reason. that's how i think about AI tools too — don't use everything just because you can. pick the ones that actually matter. set them up well. let them do their job.

less waste. more purpose. more time for the stuff that actually matters.

that feels sustainable to me.
